# Tolerance audit

Generated by `docs/plans/generate_tolerance_audit.py` from the committed fixtures. Every number below is `testdata/golden/{id}.png` against `testdata/matplotlib_ref/{id}.png` under the harness metric.

## Summary

- Cases audited: **190** (every catalog row has both a golden and a reference).
- Pixel-identical (within 1 LSB): **29**.
- Residual is a dominant integer pixel offset: **3** — placement bugs, not amplitude noise.
- Sparse local residual (<1% of frame): **129**.
- Dense low-amplitude wash (p99 <= 32): **15**.
- Dense high-amplitude residual: **14**.
- `MaxRMSE` at least 3x the measured value: **52**.
- `MaxRMSE` under 1.15x the measured value: **20**.
- PSNR is no longer a gate anywhere. Fixing the `uint8` overflow in `imagecmp`'s PSNR accumulator made `PSNR == 20*log10(255/RMSE)` exact and every `MinPSNR` floor a restatement of `MaxRMSE`. `Case.MinPSNR` is gone; the 60 floors that were both binding and reachable were folded into `MaxRMSE`, and the 65 that the overflow had made unreachable were dropped, to be re-derived from measurement.

## Why RMSE alone is not a gate

`basic_line` is the smallest plot in the catalog, and it is why this table exists. Its residual used to be 139 pixels out of 230,400 — a single y tick label rendered one pixel low, with glyph rasterization otherwise byte-identical. Those 139 pixels reached a per-channel difference of 249 and still produced RMSE 2.49, comfortably inside the case's 2.8 allowance. Whole-image RMSE dilutes a fully-wrong glyph by frame area, so it cannot bound a localized placement error; the case now measures zero differing pixels, and only the shape gate would have caught the regression that hid there. The `family` column below exists to separate such cases from genuine antialiasing washes.

## Shift families

Cases whose largest residual clusters are cancelled by one integer offset. Each offset is a candidate shared root cause.

- **shift +0+1** (2): `formatter_engineering_labels`, `lines_markers_gallery`
- **shift -1+0** (1): `formatter_scalar_scientific_labels`
